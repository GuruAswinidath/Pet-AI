# Kitten Care Basics

## Overview

The first year of a cat's life involves a predictable sequence of developmental
milestones and preventive care visits. Most of the questions new kitten owners ask
- about teething, growth, vaccines, and when to spay/neuter - have fairly
standard answers, which makes this good ground for general reference material
rather than urgent triage.

## Teething

Kittens are born without teeth. Baby (deciduous) teeth begin coming in around 2-4
weeks of age, and the full set of 26 baby teeth is usually in by about 6-8 weeks.
Losing those baby teeth as the 30 adult teeth come in is a completely normal
process that typically happens between roughly 3 and 7 months of age - it's common
for owners to never actually find the lost teeth, since kittens often swallow them
without issue while eating. Mild gum irritation, some drooling, or a preference for
softer food during this window is normal. It only becomes a concern if a baby tooth
is still present alongside the adult tooth coming in behind it ("retained
deciduous teeth"), which can require a vet to remove it, or if there's persistent
bleeding, swelling, or the kitten stops eating.

## Vaccination and Deworming Timeline

A typical kitten vaccine series (confirm exact timing with a vet, as protocols vary
by region and risk factors):

- Roughly 6-8 weeks: first combination vaccine (commonly covering feline
  rhinotracheitis, calicivirus, and panleukopenia - often called FVRCP)
- Boosters roughly every 3-4 weeks until about 16 weeks of age
- Rabies vaccine, timing depends on local regulations
- Feline leukemia virus (FeLV) vaccine is often recommended for kittens,
  particularly if there's any chance of outdoor access
- Deworming is typically done on a repeated schedule through the first few months,
  since kittens commonly pick up intestinal parasites from their mother or
  environment

## Growth and Weight

Kittens grow quickly and gain weight steadily through their first year, reaching
close to adult size by around 9-12 months (large breeds may take longer). A kitten
that stops gaining weight, loses weight, or shows a sudden drop in appetite is more
concerning than the same signs in an adult cat, given how little reserve a small
body has - see the "Loss of appetite" and "Vomiting/Diarrhoea" reference docs for
the urgency thresholds.

## Spaying/Neutering

Spay/neuter is commonly performed around 4-6 months of age, though some shelters
and vets now recommend earlier ("early-age") sterilization. Benefits typically
cited include prevention of unwanted litters, reduced roaming/marking behavior,
and lower risk of certain reproductive cancers later in life.

## Common Questions Owners Ask

- "Is it normal for my kitten to lose its baby teeth?" - Yes, this is a normal part
  of development between roughly 3 and 7 months; it only needs a vet visit if a
  baby tooth doesn't fall out on its own or if there's significant bleeding/pain.
- "When should my kitten be fully vaccinated?" - Usually by around 16 weeks, after
  completing the full booster series.
- "How do I know if my kitten's growth is on track?" - Steady, gradual weight gain
  and increasing activity/coordination are the general signs; a vet visit can
  confirm growth against a standard curve.

## When to See a Vet

Routine kitten wellness visits are the norm for this topic, not emergencies. Treat
it as urgent instead if a kitten stops eating, has diarrhoea or vomiting lasting
more than a day, seems unusually lethargic, or has any of the red-flag symptoms
described elsewhere in this knowledge base - kittens have much less physical
reserve than adult cats and can decline faster.
