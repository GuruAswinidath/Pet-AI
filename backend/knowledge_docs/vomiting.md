# Vomiting in Cats

## Overview

Cats vomit more readily than dogs, and an occasional hairball or a single episode
after eating too fast is usually not a cause for concern. The important distinction
for triage is between an isolated, mild episode in a cat that's otherwise acting
normal, versus repeated vomiting, vomiting combined with other symptoms, or a
chronic low-grade pattern that owners sometimes dismiss as "just hairballs" when it
is actually a sign of underlying disease.

## Symptoms

- Bringing up hair, undigested food, foam, or clear/yellow fluid (bile)
- Retching or gagging before producing vomit
- Repeated vomiting within a short window
- Vomiting alongside reduced appetite, lethargy, or diarrhea
- Blood in the vomit, or a "coffee-ground" appearance (a sign of digested blood)
- A chronic pattern of vomiting more than once or twice a month over an extended time

## Questions to Ask

- Does the vomit contain hair, food, or fluid - and what color?
- How many times has this happened, and over what time period?
- Is the cat still eating, drinking, and behaving normally between episodes?
- Any blood, or a coffee-ground appearance?
- Has this been happening on and off for weeks or months, even if infrequent?

## Red Flags

- Blood in the vomit or a coffee-ground appearance
- Three or more episodes within a few hours
- Vomiting combined with lethargy or not eating for a full day
- Unable to keep water down
- A distended or bloated abdomen alongside vomiting

## Typical Triage Level

**Varies** - this is the symptom most dependent on pattern and context. A single mild
episode with a cat that's otherwise normal is usually fine to monitor at home for a
few hours. Anything with blood, repeated episodes, or accompanying symptoms should be
seen the same day.

## Owner Guidance

For a mild, isolated episode: withhold food for a few hours (not water), then offer a
small, bland meal and watch for repeat episodes. For anything with blood, repeated
vomiting, or vomiting paired with lethargy or not eating, seek same-day veterinary
care. Importantly, a cat vomiting more than a couple of times a month on an ongoing
basis - even if each episode looks unremarkable - is not "just a hairball cat"; that
pattern is often the first visible sign of chronic conditions such as inflammatory
bowel disease, hyperthyroidism, or kidney disease, and is worth a non-urgent vet
visit to investigate rather than being written off indefinitely.
