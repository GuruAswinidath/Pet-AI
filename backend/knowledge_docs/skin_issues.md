# Skin Issues in Cats (Itching, Overgrooming, Hair Loss, Rash)

## Overview

Skin problems are among the most common non-emergency reasons cat owners seek care.
The most frequent underlying causes are fleas and flea allergy dermatitis, food or
environmental allergies, and stress-related overgrooming. Cats are meticulous
groomers, which means the visible sign of a skin problem is often not a rash itself
but hair loss or raw skin from a cat excessively licking or chewing at an itchy area
- owners may never actually see the cat scratching.

## Symptoms

- Visible scratching, licking, or chewing at a specific area
- Hair loss, especially in a symmetric pattern (belly, inner thighs) suggestive of
  overgrooming rather than a primary skin disease
- Small scabs scattered across the body (a pattern called miliary dermatitis, often
  flea-related)
- Redness, flaking, or greasy patches
- Raw or broken skin from excessive licking or scratching

## Questions to Ask

- Is it in one spot or spread across the body?
- Is the cat licking or grooming that area more than usual?
- Any visible fleas or flea dirt (small dark specks in the fur), and is the cat on
  flea prevention?
- Any recent change in food, litter brand, or household cleaning products?
- Is the cat indoor-only, or do they spend time outside?
- Any recent stressors that could point to psychogenic (stress-related) overgrooming?

## Red Flags

- Sudden facial swelling
- Hives combined with breathing trouble (signs of a serious allergic reaction)
- An open wound that is spreading or not healing
- Raw, broken skin from grooming

## Typical Triage Level

**Home to soon.** Most skin issues are not urgent and can be scheduled as a routine
vet visit. The exception is any sign of an acute allergic reaction (facial swelling,
hives with breathing changes), which is an emergency.

## Owner Guidance

Prevent excessive licking or scratching where possible - an Elizabethan collar
("cone") can help if the skin is already raw - and keep the area clean and dry.
Confirm the cat is on a reliable flea preventive, since flea allergy is the single
most common treatable cause of itchy skin in cats. Persistent itching, patchy hair
loss, or scattered scabs are worth a routine (non-emergency) vet visit to identify
the underlying cause. Sudden facial swelling or any breathing changes alongside skin
symptoms should be treated as an emergency rather than monitored.
