# Litter Box Behavior and House-Soiling

## Overview

House-soiling (urinating or defecating outside the litter box) is one of the most
common behavioral complaints from cat owners - but it's also one of the areas where
distinguishing a *behavioral/environmental* cause from a *medical emergency* matters
most. This document covers the behavioral/setup side; if there's any possibility a
cat is straining to urinate and producing little or nothing, treat that as the
urinary obstruction emergency described in this knowledge base's triage material,
not as a litter box preference issue.

## Common Non-Medical Causes

- **Litter box setup** - too few boxes (the general guideline many vets suggest is
  one box per cat plus one extra), boxes that are too small, covered boxes some
  cats dislike, or boxes placed in noisy/high-traffic areas
- **Litter type or cleanliness** - some cats are particular about litter texture or
  scent, and infrequently cleaned boxes are a common deterrent
- **Multi-cat household stress** - competition for box access, or one cat
  "guarding" a box, can lead another cat to avoid it
- **Location changes** - moving a box, moving house, or new furniture/pets can
  disrupt a previously reliable routine
- **Marking behavior** - distinct from house-soiling; territorial urine marking
  (often small amounts on vertical surfaces) has different underlying causes,
  commonly linked to stress or an unneutered/unspayed status

## Questions to Consider Before Assuming It's Behavioral

- Is the cat producing a normal amount of urine outside the box, or straining with
  little/nothing coming out? (The latter is the urinary obstruction emergency, not
  a behavior issue - see this knowledge base's urinary obstruction material.)
- Is this new, or has the cat always had access issues (e.g. a new multi-cat
  household)?
- Has anything changed recently - new litter, new box location, a new pet, a move?
- Is the cat otherwise acting completely normal, eating and drinking well?

## Common Questions Owners Ask

- "How many litter boxes do I need?" - A commonly cited general guideline is one
  box per cat plus one extra, spread across different locations rather than
  clustered together.
- "My cat suddenly stopped using the litter box - is this behavioral?" - A sudden
  change, especially with straining, crying, or reduced urine volume, should be
  checked for a medical cause first before assuming it's behavioral - especially
  in male cats, given the urinary obstruction risk.
- "Does neutering help with marking?" - Neutering/spaying often reduces
  territorial marking behavior, particularly if done before the behavior becomes
  well-established.

## When to See a Vet

Litter box avoidance with normal-looking urination and an otherwise well cat is
reasonable to address with environmental/behavioral changes first. Escalate
immediately to the same-day urinary obstruction emergency pathway if there's any
straining, crying, frequent unproductive trips to the box, or you simply can't
tell whether normal urine is being produced - when in doubt, treat it as the
medical emergency rather than the behavioral issue.
